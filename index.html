What Nate Silver said

From Silver Bulletin posts and the Still Counting podcast with Galen Druke and Clare Malone (latest episode: “Final Midterms Thoughts + Haley Stevens’ Heel Turn”).

Texas is the hinge

If Talarico wins, Democrats flip the Senate about 90% of the time in FLIPR Deluxe. If he loses, they still have decent odds.

The Obama-states path

Democrats can flip the Senate winning only states Obama carried at least once: North Carolina, Maine, Ohio and Iowa, while holding all their own seats. Exotic extras: Alaska, Kansas, Nebraska, South Carolina, maybe Montana.

No safe space

There’s no ‘Blue Wall’ or anything like that. Except probably for North Carolina, none of the prospective D pickups are ‘safe’ or ‘solid.’

How much polling error Democrats can absorb

His Oct 17 stress test: polls off by 2 points against Democrats everywhere still gives a 52–48 Democratic Senate. At 3 points Maine and Texas flip back and Democrats top out at 50. At 4 points, Republicans hold 52–48.

Midterms are less correlated

Results may not move in lockstep. I wouldn’t really be that surprised, for instance, if Talarico wins — but Susan Collins wins too.

Reading the night, hour by hour

Built from the commentary above plus Decision Desk HQ and NPR. These are guides, not predictions, and closing times are approximate. Wait for AP calls.

6:00 PM

Kentucky (most), Indiana (most). KY-6 around Lexington is open, Trump +10, and both parties spent six figures there late. A close count is the first hint of how deep the House wave runs.

7:00 PM

Georgia, Virginia, South Carolina, most of New Hampshire and Florida. DDHQ treats VA-2 (Luria vs. Kiggans) as an early bellwether. Ossoff comfortably over 50% fits a polling environment that is holding. Silver’s SC rule: if Democrats win there, they have likely already won Texas and the Senate.Georgia needs 50% to avoid a January runoff.

7:30 PM

North Carolina, Ohio. Cooper is the one pickup Silver calls close to safe; a tight race would be a warning sign. A Brown win in Ohio keeps the Obama-states path alive.

8:00 PM

Maine, Michigan, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, most of Texas. Texas is the hinge. In Michigan, watch whether El-Sayed runs well behind the governor’s race after Stevens’ endorsement of Rogers. Maine may split from the national trend in either direction.

9:00 PM

Iowa, Kansas, Minnesota, Nebraska, New York, Arizona, Colorado, Wisconsin. Iowa is the fourth seat on the path that skips Texas. Kansas or Nebraska turning blue would be a blowout. Minnesota is the hold Silver has worried about.

10 PM – late

Montana, then California, Washington, Alaska. West Coast mail ballots count slowly and Alaska uses ranked-choice voting, so the House margin and Alaska may take days.
